Self-organising digital circuits — JAX to FPGA

A circuit is a layered DAG of differentiable LUTs, configured by a local message-passing rule meta-learned once offline. At deployment it runs forward passes only: when a cosmic ray scrambles LUT config bits, neighbouring gates negotiate a configuration that computes the target function again. Everything on the FPGA itself, including the update rule.

Operation under errors

All three fault models on one page per circuit. Soft errors: one scrambled LUT step by step, recovery against TMR / scrubbing / ECC / genetic repair, every method under a storm. Hard errors: cells frozen at coin flips that reject every write — only the searching methods survive, and the wall past which the task no longer fits the free bits. Parametric drift: dose shifts every gate's analog margin, no bit is ever wrong, scrubbing and ECC are structurally blind — pre-registered, predictions scored against the run.

  • adder2 under errors60 gates, the 898-parameter rule: repairs 40 scrambled gates exactly on the circuit it grew.
  • adder4 under errors264 gates, the 63k-parameter rule against the storm knee the scale question cares about.
  • mul3 under errors360 gates, the multiplier — where the host GA collapsed on soft errors and the fabric GA did not.
  • mul4 under errors704 gates, 11,264 configuration bits — the scale where the genetic search starts needing thousands of generations and the rule repairs 40 scrambled gates to 0.999.
  • gf4 under errors704 gates, carry-free GF(2⁴) multiply — the strongest repair result in the project: 94% exact at 40 of 640 hidden gates scrambled, and a 60-fault chain walked without a single failure.
