The deployed rule, measured on the FPGA

The quant-8bit policy — same file, same 62,306 weights — compiled to a complete ULX3S design and measured where it will run: RTL simulation of the actual bitstream sources, cycle for cycle, with the substrate evaluating itself, the error counters sensing, and the rule repairing. Every state byte is checked against the integer model at every step; agreement is exact, so the model's statistics are the chip's.

Sources: soc.qcosim --episode --steps 12 --lanes 34 (iverilog), apio report (nextpnr placement + timing), soc.bandwidth --rtl (traffic counters in the design). Full story: docs/deployment.md.

How much does error correction slow the circuit down?

healthywhile repairingamortised
parallel substrate (this build, soc_qfab) 1 add/clock · 25M add/s; a full self-check is 261 cycles 1.0× — no shared resource; repair writes config registers the cost is the 0.27 s window in which outputs are wrong; repair closes it
interpreted substrate (soc.qrtl --no-fabric, the iCE40 concession) 521 cyc/add · 48k add/s 5,691 cyc/add — 10.9× slower 0.10% at one fault/hour

Bandwidth split, from the design's own counters: the circuit moves 35.8 KB per exhaustive evaluation (143 B per addition); error correction moves 815 KB per step — 90% of it re-reading the 72.8 KB weight stream once per tile — and 51 MB per 64-step repair. On this build's parallel substrate the circuit moves nothing — the memory system is the error-correction subsystem, and its whole bill is that 51 MB + 3.4 s per fault event.
